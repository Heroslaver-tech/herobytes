How the debt snowball works

And when the avalanche is the better choice.

With the debt snowball you pay the minimum on every debt and put every extra dollar on the smallest balance. When it is gone, its minimum joins the extra money and rolls into the next smallest debt, and so on. The payment grows like a snowball, and the quick early wins keep you going.

With the debt avalanche the extra money goes to the highest interest rate first. You pay less interest in total, but the first debt may take longer to disappear. The planner calculates both on your real numbers, so you can see the difference in months and in money before choosing.

  1. Settings: your currency code, the month you start, the extra amount you can pay each month on top of the minimums, and the method: Snowball, Avalanche or Custom.
  2. Debts: up to 10 debts with today's balance, APR and minimum payment. A warning appears if a minimum does not even cover that month's interest.
  3. Read the result: payoff month and date per debt, interest paid per debt, your debt-free date and the total interest. Change the extra payment and watch the date move.
  4. Payment log: record what you really pay, to compare with the plan.

Can I do this with a blank spreadsheet?

For one debt, yes. For several debts with a rolling payment it gets hard quickly: every month each debt gets its interest and minimum, the money left over has to flow to the next debt in priority order, and a debt that finishes mid-month frees part of a payment. The template does this for 120 months and 10 debts, with formulas that work in Excel and Google Sheets in any language.

Questions

Snowball or avalanche: which one should I pick?

If staying motivated is the hard part, snowball. If you only care about paying the least interest, avalanche. The difference is often smaller than people think; the planner shows it on your own debts.

Which Excel versions does it work in?

Microsoft Excel 2010 or newer, on Windows and Mac, in any language and with a decimal point or a decimal comma.

Does it work in Google Sheets?

Yes. The download includes a PDF with a link that makes your own copy in Google Sheets in one click.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is a calculator for the plan you choose. The figures depend on the balances, rates and payments you type.
