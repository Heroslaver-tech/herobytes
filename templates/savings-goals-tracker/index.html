How sinking funds work

Small monthly amounts instead of one big bill.

A sinking fund is money you set aside every month for an expense you already know is coming: car insurance in September, gifts in December, a vacation in July. Divide what is left by the months until the date and you know your monthly amount. Do it for six goals at once, with money going in and sometimes coming out, and a spreadsheet does it better than your head.

  1. Settings: your currency code and, if you like, how much you can save per month for all goals together.
  2. Goals: one row per goal with the target, the date you need it (optional), what you already saved and what you plan to put in each month.
  3. Deposits: a row each time you move money into a goal, or take it out (Type = Withdrawal). A check flags a goal name that does not exist.
  4. Read the result: progress bar, months left, the amount needed per month, the month you will finish at your plan and a status: On track, Behind, Overdue or Done. The summary compares what your dates need with your monthly budget.
  5. By Month: what you really saved per goal in each month, against your budget. Months below budget turn red.

How is "needed per month" counted?

Months left includes the current month: a goal due in December, seen in October, has three months left (October, November, December). What is still missing is divided by those months. When the date has passed and the goal is not complete, the whole remaining amount is needed now and the goal shows Overdue.
